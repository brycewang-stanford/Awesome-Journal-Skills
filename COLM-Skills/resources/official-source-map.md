# Official Source Map - COLM

Access date: 2026-07-08. Submission rules, LLM policy, reciprocal reviewing, camera-ready
and attendance rules, and the organizing committee **re-verified 2026-11-23** by direct
fetch of the live pages on the new official site `colm.cc` (CFP, Dates, Submission
Instructions, Author Guide, FAQ, Organizers) plus `proceedings.colm.cc`.

**Edition status, checked 2026-11-23.** COLM 2026 has met (Hilton Union Square, San
Francisco, October 6-9, 2026; pre-proceedings posted at `proceedings.colm.cc`), and it is
still the newest edition COLM has published. No call, date or location for the 2027 edition exists
on any official surface: `colm.cc/Conferences/2027` returns 404, the `colm.cc` homepage
covers only 2026 (with 2025 and 2024 as past editions), and a web search for the next
edition finds only a third-party aggregator saying its dates and venue are unannounced.
Treat every 2026 date below as historical and the 2026 rules as the most recent stated
ones; an author planning for the next cycle must be told there is no published deadline
yet, not handed the 2026 calendar a year older.

**Site move (2026-11-23).** `colmweb.org` now serves only a notice that the site "has
moved" to https://colm.cc/ (the 2024 and 2025 archive pages under `colmweb.org/2024/`
and `colmweb.org/2025/` still load). The 2026 pages live under
`colm.cc/Conferences/2026/…`; the old `colmweb.org/*.html` 2026 URLs below are kept as
the July 2026 provenance and superseded by the `colm.cc` rows. The OpenReview group ID
`colmweb.org/COLM/2026/Conference` is an identifier, not a web page, and is unchanged.

This map records the official sources behind every COLM-specific fact in this pack.
COLM is a young venue — its first edition ran in 2024 — and its policies are still
being written edition by edition. Reopen the current `colm.cc` Call for Papers,
Dates page, submission instructions, author guide, and FAQ before giving any
deadline-sensitive advice.

**Access-method note (2026-07-08; superseded 2026-11-23):** in July, direct fetches of
`colmweb.org` and `dblp.org` returned HTTP 403 through the network gateway used for
verification, so official-page contents were read through search-engine renderings of
the exact URLs listed below, cross-checked against the COLM OpenReview groups, the
conference's public announcements, and independent institutional pages (e.g., the Johns
Hopkins CS department's award coverage). On 2026-11-23 the `colm.cc` pages (rows 13-19)
answered direct fetches and were read directly.

## Verified 2026-cycle facts used in the skills

Re-verified 2026-11-23 against sources 13-21 unless a line says otherwise.

- COLM 2026 is the third edition of the Conference on Language Modeling, held at the
  Hilton Union Square, San Francisco, USA, October 6-9, 2026 (main conference
  October 6-8, workshops October 9). It has met; it is still the newest edition
  published (sources 13, 15).
- The 2026 main-track timeline: abstract deadline March 26; full paper March 31;
  reviews released May 22; author rebuttal May 22 - June 8; decisions July 8
  (sources 14, 15); camera-ready August 7 (read on the July 2026 dates page, source 2;
  the live Dates page no longer lists a camera-ready date). All historical now.
- Submissions run entirely on OpenReview
  (`colmweb.org/COLM/2026/Conference`); the venue opened for submissions in
  February 2026. Papers are public on OpenReview, and COLM also posts
  pre-proceedings at `proceedings.colm.cc` (COLM 2026 = Volume 3; source 20).
- Format: "a strict upper limit of 9 pages for the main text"; unlimited additional
  pages for citations; unlimited appendices that reviewers are not required to read;
  an optional ethics statement, an optional reproducibility statement, and an optional
  acknowledgment section may each run at most 1 page outside the limit (source 14).
  Review is double-blind, so submissions carry no acknowledgments and no
  identity-revealing links (e.g., GitHub). Template: the COLM 2026 LaTeX release; no
  package may alter template parameters (sources 14, 16). No abstract word limit is
  stated.
- No person may be an author on more than 25 submissions (source 14).
- Supplementary code/data: one anonymized zip of at most 50 MB, or an anonymous URL
  (source 17).
- Every author needs an up-to-date OpenReview profile, and all authors must
  explicitly acknowledge the COLM Code of Ethics at submission (sources 16, 17).
- Reciprocal reviewing has per-submission and per-reviewer components; authors with
  4+ submissions join the reviewer pool automatically; distinct submissions may not
  reuse the same reciprocal reviewer except as a last resort; reviewers can expect up
  to 5 papers (sources 14, 18).
- The 2026 LLM-usage policy ("COLM 2026 will adopt the ICLR 2026 Policy on Large
  Language Model Usage", relaxed for minor assistance) requires disclosure of
  LLM-originated ideas, LLM-written content (including references), LLM-generated
  data/plots, and LLM-based evaluation; grammar checkers, typo fixes and coding
  assistants need no disclosure; reviewers disclose any LLM use (sources 14, 16).
- Dual submission: a COLM submission may not be under review at another archival
  venue (ICML papers withdrawn, ARR papers at any stage excluded) by the March 31
  deadline; non-anonymous preprints are permitted (sources 16, 18).
- Camera-ready: accepted papers may add **one extra page** for content responding to
  reviewer and AC feedback (9 + 1 pages of main text); authors cannot be added or
  removed (source 18).
- Attendance: at least one author of each accepted paper must register for the main
  conference (workshop registration does not count); COLM is in-person only, with no
  remote presentation (source 18).
- COLM 2026 organizers: General Chair Yoav Artzi (Cornell University / Google
  DeepMind); Senior Program Chair Greg Durrett (New York University); Program Chairs
  Yulia Tsvetkov (UW), Aviral Kumar (CMU), and Yoon Kim (MIT) (source 19). Chairs
  rotate per edition.
- Editions to date: 2024 Philadelphia (University of Pennsylvania), 2025 Montreal
  (Palais des Congrès, source 21), 2026 San Francisco. Each of the first two editions
  gave four Outstanding Paper Awards.
- COLM was announced publicly in October 2023 as a venue dedicated to the theory,
  practice, and applications of language models; the founding motivation, per the
  official site, is that LM technology raises research questions distinct from the
  typical focus of adjacent venues.
