## Verified ISSTA 2027 facts used in skills

- ISSTA 2027 is the 36th ACM SIGSOFT International Symposium on Software Testing and Analysis,
  Singapore, September 7-10, 2027 (source 3). General Chair Jun Sun; Program Co-Chairs Dan Hao
  and Jacques Klein (source 4).
- Submission is through HotCRP (`issta2027.hotcrp.com`); accepted papers are published as
  journal articles in **PACMSE**, Issue ISSTA 2027, in the ACM Digital Library — the same
  model ISSTA 2025 and 2026 used (sources 1, 6).
- Dates (AoE): mandatory abstract January 8, 2027; full paper January 11, 2027; author response
  March 22-25; initial notification including early reject April 20; major-revision submission
  May 20; final notification June 17; camera ready July 22, 2027 (source 2).
- First-round outcomes are Accept, Major Revision, or Reject. A Major Revision comes with a set of
  revision items that "form a contract" between reviewers and authors; authors have four weeks.
  Earlier editions (e.g. ISSTA 2023, 2024) ran two rolling submission deadlines — the multi-round
  model is real ISSTA history but its exact shape is cycle-specific.
- **Two-phase review (new in 2027):** each submission is first read by two reviewers; if both are
  negative it is rejected early, otherwise a third reviewer is added. Each paper therefore gets
  two or three PC reviews (more on disagreement), handled by an area chair. (ISSTA 2026 promised
  at least three.)
- **Submission cap (new in 2027):** at most 7 submissions per author per edition.
- Three submission kinds: Research Papers, Experience Papers, Replicability Studies; the latter two
  add "(Experience Paper)" or "(Replicability Study)" to the title. Each paper chooses one primary
  (and optionally a secondary) research area out of eight.
- Format: ACM `acmart` with `acmsmall` (single-column), `screen`, `review`, `anonymous`; 18 pages
  for everything except references and the Data Availability section — every section, figure and
  appendix counts; exceeding it is a desk rejection. (Older editions used the two-column `sigconf`
  format with 10 pages plus 2 for references; earlier versions of this pack wrongly carried
  `sigconf` forward — the 2026 call already used `acmsmall`.)
- A **Data Availability** section before the references is required (not counted), and the
  submission must link an anonymous artifact or explain why not; at least one reviewer checks it.
  Sharing is not mandatory for acceptance but informs the decision.
- Reviewing is double-anonymous; self-citation in the third person; check PDF metadata.
- Generative AI: ACM Policy on Authorship (updated May 14, 2026) — AI used in the research itself
  must be described in the methods section; AI used only to assist writing need not be disclosed;
  fabricated or hallucinated citations lead to desk rejection; prompt injection is prohibited.
- Criteria: verifiability and transparency, and presentation, for all papers; plus importance,
  originality and soundness (research), importance and scope / insights and evidence / perspective
  (experience), comprehensiveness / validity / perspective / artifact availability (replicability).
- Artifact evaluation (latest posted: ISSTA 2026, source 8) is voluntary and post-acceptance,
  awarding up to two ACM badges among Functional, Reusable and Available; "Available" needs a
  DOI-issuing archive (Zenodo, FigShare, Dryad or similar), not a bare GitHub link. The ISSTA 2027
  call says positively evaluated replicability-study artifacts are eligible for the Results
  Reproduced badge, while the 2026 AE call says Results Validated badges are not awarded through
  AE — 待核实 until the 2027 AE call appears.

## Organizers and fee model

- ISSTA is a conference series, run by ACM SIGSOFT. Like every conference in this collection it has
  NO standing editor-in-chief; the rotating analogue is the per-edition General Chair and Program
  Co-Chairs, appointed by SIGSOFT/the steering committee and turning over each year.
- **Open access with a possible APC.** Since 2026 all ACM articles are open access. Authors
  covered by an ACM OPEN institutional agreement pay no APC; others may have to pay one (waivers
  and discounts exist). The 2027 fee is "to be announced"; the 2026 fee was US$250 for ACM members
  and US$350 for non-members, payable at camera-ready (source 1). Registration is a separate cost.
  (Corrects the earlier "no APC" statement in this pack.)
