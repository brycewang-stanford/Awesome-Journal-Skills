## Verified AAAI-27 facts used in skills

- The main technical track uses OpenReview and double-blind review. Authors must use the same
  OpenReview account across all submissions and review assignments.
- AAAI-27 has three technical tracks: Main Track, AI for Social Impact, and AI Alignment. The
  10-submission author limit counts the main technical track and excludes the two special tracks.
- The Main Track uses a two-phase review process. Phase 1 allocates three reviewers — two human
  reviews supplemented by one non-decisional AI-generated review; papers with sufficiently negative
  reviews are rejected without author feedback. Papers continuing to Phase 2 receive additional
  reviews, up to five in total, and the Phase 2 reviewers are not shown the Phase 1 reviews until
  they have submitted their own. One author feedback phase follows, then metareview and disposition.
- Submissions use AAAI two-column camera-ready style and US Letter PDF. The main PDF may run to
  **9 pages, with pages 8-9 reserved exclusively for references** — that is, 7 pages of
  non-reference content. Acknowledgements are omitted at review time, and any ethical statement
  must fit **inside** the 7 content pages.
- The reproducibility checklist is completed from the Author Kit and **uploaded separately from the
  main paper**, in its own field on the submission form, and is shown to reviewers. This changed
  from AAAI-26, where the checklist sat inside the PDF after the references.
- Reviewers assess reproducibility from what is actually submitted, and this contributes to the
  decision. Material promised "after acceptance or publication" must not be treated as evidence
  that it exists.
- Supplementary material is due three days after the paper deadline (2026-07-31) in three
  categories: a Supplementary Document (PDF), a Supplementary Media Archive (ZIP), and a
  Supplementary Code and Data Package (ZIP). Pointers to material on the web are disallowed —
  **including anonymous repositories and anonymous dataset hosts** — because they can violate both
  anonymity and immutability. Reviewers are not obliged to consult any of it, so anything critical
  belongs in the main paper.
- Authors are limited to 10 combined main-technical-track submissions, may not be added to papers
  after submission, and may modify submissions only until the full-paper deadline.
- Simultaneous submission to another archival venue is prohibited, and the policy now names the
  patterns it covers: cosmetic re-submission, "thin slicing" one contribution into several papers,
  splitting results across methods/datasets/domains, and obfuscated or "alternative universe"
  duplicates. Overlapping submissions by an overlapping author set may be assessed together.
- Papers published less than two months before 2026-07-28 are contemporaneous; authors are not
  obliged to address them, though doing so in the camera-ready is encouraged.
- **New in AAAI-27:** all qualified authors are expected to join the reviewer pool at a light load
  unless extenuating circumstances apply, and potential reviewers are vetted by research experience.
- AAAI policy allows LLMs for editing and polishing author-written text but prohibits LLM-generated
  manuscript text unless it is part of the experimental analysis. AI systems cannot be authors or
  citable sources.
- Accepted papers get 7 proceedings pages plus up to 2 acknowledgement/reference pages, with up to
  two further pages purchasable at $300 each. Camera-ready source files are due 2026-12-14, copyright
  transfers to AAAI, and at least one author must register at the in-person rate and present in
  person — there is no remote presentation option, and papers without it are removed before
  publication.

## Organizers and fee model

- AAAI is run by the Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence. Like all CS/AI
  conferences in this collection, it has NO standing editor-in-chief; the rotating analogue is the
  per-edition Program Chairs.
- **No AAAI-27 Program Chair names are recorded here.** 待核实 — at this access date
  `https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-27/conference-organizers/` redirects to the AAAI-25
  organizers page rather than serving an AAAI-27 roster, so no official page names the AAAI-27
  chairs. The AAAI-26 chairs were Chad Jenkins (University of Michigan) and Matthew Taylor
  (University of Alberta); those appointments rotate every cycle and must not be carried forward.
  Re-check the organizers page before relying on a name.
- There is NO article-processing charge (APC). AAAI funds the conference through registration fees:
  at least one author of an accepted paper must register at the in-person rate and present.
  Proceedings appear open-access in the AAAI Digital Library / OJS.
