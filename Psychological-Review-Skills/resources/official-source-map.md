# Official Source Map — Psychological Review

Citable sources for the Psychological Review-specific facts used across these skills.
Psychological Review is the American Psychological Association's (APA) flagship journal for
**theoretical contributions** in scientific psychology — new theory, formal/mathematical/
computational models, and major theoretical syntheses. It does **not** publish primary
empirical reports: data appear only to motivate or constrain theory. Original access dates below
are **2026-06** (检索于 2026-06；以官网为准). Submission rules, review mode, abstract/keyword
limits, TOP level, fees, AI policy and masthead **re-verified 2026-11-24** against the live APA
submission-guidelines page (https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/rev/submit, "Date created: March
2026"), the editorial-board page and the journal home page. APA's Incapsula bot wall returns an
empty challenge page to direct `curl`/WebFetch, so the live pages were **read via the r.jina.ai
reader rendering** of the same URLs (the journal home page was cross-checked against the Internet
Archive snapshot of 2026-10-24). **Change found on 2026-11-24:** the guidelines now state that
**open (unmasked) review is the default** and **masked review is an author option** — earlier
versions of this pack described review as masked. Items that could not be verified from official
sources are listed under "Still unverified" and flagged inline in the skills.

## Verified facts (with source #)

- Psychological Review publishes **theoretical contributions only**, including systematic
  evaluation of alternative theories; surveys, method/design papers, and empirical reports are
  out of scope. (Source 1)
- Article types include full **Theoretical articles** and shorter **Theoretical Notes**. (Source 1)
- No fixed upper length; **> 15,000-word** texts must be justified. (Source 1)
- Replications are publishable only when relevant to / embedded in new or enhanced theory, and
  should be mentioned in the abstract. (Source 1, secondary submission guides)
- Submission and peer review run through **Editorial Manager** (editorialmanager.com/rev). (Source 2)
- **Review mode: open (unmasked) review is the default; masked review is an author option**
  (re-verified 2026-11-24). Authors who choose masked review put names/affiliations only in the
  cover letter and remove identifying clues from the manuscript. (Source 5)
- **Abstract ≤ 250 words** on a separate page, followed by **up to five keywords**. (Source 5)
- **TOP "disclosure" level** for all eight aspects for empirical research, reported in a
  "Transparency and openness" Method subsection; preregistration status must be stated. (Source 5)
- **Generative AI:** disclose use in the method section and cite it; AI cannot be an author. (Source 5)
- **Fees:** submitting is free of charge; an APC applies only to the gold open-access option. (Source 5)
- Current editor: **Elena L. Grigorenko, PhD, University of Houston** (editor since 2022; re-verified 2026-11-24 on the APA editorial-board page). (Sources 1, 6)
- Manuscripts follow **APA Style** (7th edition, confirmed on Source 5): double-spaced, author–date
  citations; a separate anonymized title page only when masked review is chosen. (Sources 4–5)
- Published by the **American Psychological Association**. (Source 1)

## Sibling-journal guard (do not confuse with Psychological Review)

- **Psychological Bulletin** — integrative reviews and meta-analyses (synthesis of evidence), NOT
  new formal theory. A summary belongs there, not at Review.
- **Behavioral and Brain Sciences (BBS)** — target article + open peer commentary format.
- **Trends in Cognitive Sciences** — short, invited opinion/review pieces.
- **JEP family (e.g., JEP: General, JEP: LMC) / Psychological Science** — empirical reports.
- **American Psychologist** — broad APA flagship; note **Anderson (1996), "ACT: A simple theory
  of complex cognition," appeared in *American Psychologist* 51(4):355–365, NOT Psychological
  Review** — use the verified ACT-R *Psychological Review* paper (Anderson et al., 2004) instead.
- **Nosofsky, R. M. (1986), the generalized context model paper, appeared in *Journal of
  Experimental Psychology: General* 115(1):39–57 — NOT Psychological Review.** Do not list it as a
  Review exemplar.
