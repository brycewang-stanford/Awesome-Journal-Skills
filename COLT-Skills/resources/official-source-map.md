## Verified facts used across the skills

### COLT 2027 (current edition; call read 2026-11-23)

- COLT 2027 is the 40th Annual Conference on Learning Theory, held June 28 - July 2,
  2027 in Tokyo, Japan (sources 9, 10).
- One submission deadline: January 29, 2027, Anywhere on Earth. Expected rebuttal
  period March 29 to April 2, 2027; author notification on or before May 3, 2027.
- Format: the entire manuscript as one PDF; the main body is limited to 12
  PMLR-formatted pages, **excluding references and special sections such as an Ethics
  or AI statement** (the special-section exclusion is new wording in 2027); no page
  limit on references and appendices. Everything needed to substantiate the results
  must be in the submission, but contribution, novelty and significance are judged
  primarily on the main paper.
- Review is double-blind: no author names or identifying information, own prior work
  in the third person, **no acknowledgments** in the submission (add them at
  camera-ready). The area chair knows identities and may reveal them to a reviewer
  during the rebuttal period on request; the template's `[anon]` option suppresses
  author names.
- Rebuttal: initial reviews are sent to authors before decisions, with an opportunity to
  respond.
- Dual submission: no submissions substantially similar to work published, accepted, or
  submitted in parallel to peer-reviewed conferences with proceedings or to journals;
  a paper under COLT review may not go to a journal until decisions are released or it
  is withdrawn.
- Attendance: at least one author of each accepted paper must attend in person to present.
- Publication: PMLR, with the option to opt out of the proceedings in favour of a 1-page
  extended abstract pointing to an open-access archival version of the full paper.
  PMLR is open access with no author-side fee; COLT's cost model is registration.
- Best paper and best student paper awards (student award: primary contributor(s)
  full-time students at submission).
- Program Chairs: Ruth Urner and Wouter Koolen.
- Not yet published for 2027 (see 待核实): LLM policy, submission instructions and portal,
  style-file package, conference organization beyond the PCs, Open Problems call.

### COLT 2026 (previous edition; historical anchors)

- COLT 2026 was the 39th Annual Conference on Learning Theory, held June 29 - July 3,
  2026 in San Diego, USA; Program Chairs Steve Hanneke and Tor Lattimore.
- The single submission deadline was February 4, 2026, Anywhere on Earth; submissions
  went through Microsoft CMT (not OpenReview) in both the 2025 and 2026 cycles.
  Reviews were released April 3, initial author responses were due April 10, discussion
  ran April 10-20, and authors were notified May 4, 2026.
- Format: at most 12 PMLR-formatted pages of main body excluding references; no page
  limit on references and appendices; the whole manuscript is one PDF.
- 2026 LLM policy: authors may use LLMs for writing, mathematics and general research,
  are encouraged to mention unusual model contributions (an idea, an imaginative proof),
  and are responsible for correctness, including validity of references; reviewers
  must not share submissions with any LLM.
- COLT 2026 ran an Open Problems session (invited plus regular); regular open problems
  were due May 25, 2026 (AoE), up to 4 pages excluding references, title beginning
  "Open Problem:", not anonymous, submitted by email, and published in the proceedings.

### Standing facts

- Volume/editor anchors: v247 = COLT 2024 (Agrawal, Roth); v291 = COLT 2025
  (Haghtalab, Moitra). Volume editors are that edition's program chairs.
- COLT is organized under the Association for Computational Learning, and the
  conference is single-track by long-standing practice.
- COLT has a tradition of published open-problem pieces in its proceedings (verified
  instance: PMLR v65:4-7, COLT 2017); the 2025 and 2026 cycles ran Open Problems calls
  with the formats recorded under sources 7 and 11.
- COLT has no artifact-evaluation track, badges, reproducibility checklist, or
  code-submission requirement in the 2026 or 2027 calls — an absence claim, verified to
  the extent that no such mechanism appears in the checked material.
