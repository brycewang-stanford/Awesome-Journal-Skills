## Verified 2026 facts used in skills

- ACM MM 2026 is the 34th ACM International Conference on Multimedia, held November 10–14,
  2026 in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, organized under ACM SIGMM (sources 1, 17).
- Submissions were made through OpenReview; every author needed a complete OpenReview
  profile (an incomplete co-author profile is grounds for desk rejection), and by submitting
  authors automatically volunteered to review (source 3).
- The 2026 timetable (AoE): abstract March 25, 2026; regular paper April 1, 2026;
  supplementary material April 8, 2026; rebuttal June 4, 2026; author notification July 9,
  2026 on the Important-Dates and Dataset pages (July 7, 2026 on the CFP page); camera-ready
  August 6, 2026; author registration August 31, 2026 (sources 2, 3, 8).
- Regular papers were 6–8 pages of body in the ACM `sigconf` template, with references
  permitted on up to 2 additional pages that must contain only references; overlength papers
  were desk-rejected without review. Appendices may not follow the main paper in the
  submission file: everything beyond the paper goes into the optional supplementary
  material, capped at 50Mb (sources 3, 15).
- Submissions must carry ACM CCS Concepts and Keywords ("Please remember to add Concepts and
  Keywords") (source 15).
- The main track and most tracks used double-blind review; the Reproducibility, Open Source
  Software, and Dataset tracks were single-blind because the artifact cannot be anonymized
  (sources 8, 15).
- At most 10 submissions per author across the Technical, Brave New Ideas and Datasets
  tracks jointly; the 11th and later are desk-rejected (sources 3, 8).
- Desk-reject triggers also include an author-reviewer's late or poor review, clear evidence
  of AI-generated reviews, and an explicit or hidden prompt in the paper aimed at automatic
  AI reviewing (source 3). No author-side generative-AI disclosure statement is required
  on the 2026 pages.
- The main track was organized into sixteen topics (see source 4); a submission selects a
  topic area.
- ACM MM 2026 ran multiple tracks: Main, Dataset, Brave New Ideas, Multimedia Grand
  Challenge, Open Source Software Competition, Reproducibility, Interactive Art, Doctoral
  Symposium, workshops, tutorials, panels, and demos.
- An optional anonymous rebuttal was offered after reviews, without links to external
  material; after the process concludes, the final reviews and any author responses are
  made public on OpenReview (source 3).
- ACM MM 2026 was on-site only: papers not presented in person by an author are treated as
  no-shows and removed from the proceedings (source 16).
- Accepted papers are published in the ACM Digital Library, Open Access (source 16).

## Organizers and fee model

- ACM MM is the flagship conference of ACM SIGMM (the Special Interest Group on Multimedia).
  Like every CS/AI conference in this collection, it has NO standing editor-in-chief; the
  rotating analogue is the per-edition General Chairs and Program Chairs (2026 names in
  source 17), rotating yearly. Re-check the current organization page before relying on a
  name.
- **Fee model changed with ACM's Open Access transition.** From January 1, 2026 every ACM
  publication, ACM MM proceedings included, is Open Access. Authors at an ACM Open
  institution pay no article-processing charge; authors from non-participating
  institutions pay an APC unless they qualify for a waiver, at a 2026 transition rate of
  $250 for ACM/SIG members and $350 for non-members (source 16). Registration is a separate
  cost: at least one author must register (author registration deadline August 31, 2026)
  and present on-site. The sigconf rights block is settled at camera-ready.
