# Simulated referee panel (inline panel): TEXT-BP trial, target JAMA Original Investigation

> This is a rehearsal. It predicts the attack surface and the likely decision band. It does not replace peer review, and it does not predict acceptance.
> Orchestration: **inline panel**. One agent played the AE, R1, R2, R3 and the verifier in turn. Each referee report was written before the next lens was opened, but the referees may still anchor on each other.

## Calibration

- **Venue / family:** JAMA Original Investigation, randomized clinical trial, **form E (clinical medicine)**. Calibrated 2026-11-23 from `review-forms.md` §E and the JAMA-Skills pack (`jama-statistics`, `jama-study-design`, `jama-reporting-standards`, `jama-ethics-registration`, `jama-submission`, `jama-peer-review-revision`). `resources/official-source-map.md` covers only the masthead, access model and ISSN (checked 2026-06-23). It gives no acceptance-rate figure, so this panel does not quote one.
- **Bar that applies:** JAMA runs a dedicated statistical review for manuscripts under serious consideration. That review checks four things: the primary outcome matches the registry and protocol, ITT is the primary analysis, missing data are handled by imputation with sensitivity analyses, and the conclusions stay within what the primary outcome shows (no spin). Registration before first enrolment, a named IRB, ICMJE disclosures and a data-sharing statement are hard gates (`jama-ethics-registration`). The reporting guideline is CONSORT; form E lists CONSORT 2025 as current.
- **Lenses:** R1 clinical relevance and design · R2 statistics · R3 reporting and ethics.
- **Preflight (stage 0):** verdict NO-GO. It found REF-MISSING (Margolis 2013), LEFTOVER-MARKER ("TODO: add electronic pill-cap substudy", line 123), EXH-NUMBERING (Tables 1, 2, 4), EXH-UNREFERENCED (Table 4), EXH-SE-NOTE (Table 2), NUM-MISMATCH ("5.4" in the abstract) and REF-UNCITED (REDCap). The referees treat these as mechanics the author still has to fix. They do not count them as substantive concerns, with one exception: the abstract's headline estimate is reviewed for what it means statistically (R2-M3).

## 1. AE desk screen

- **Scope / fit:** Strong. This is a pragmatic, multicentre, attention-controlled RCT (n = 1,208) of a scalable adherence intervention in federally qualified health centres. The population has low control rates and is relevant to equity. JAMA has published the closest comparator (Chow et al., JAMA 2015), and the question matters for US primary care.
- **General interest:** Moderate. The effect is modest (−4.2 mm Hg), the trial ran in one network in one state, and follow-up is 12 months with BP as the outcome. JAMA Internal Medicine or JAMA Network Open is a realistic alternative if the editors judge the advance incremental (`jama-scope-fit`).
- **Fatal-flaw scan:** Several statements about the primary outcome, the analysis population and outcome masking contradict each other between the Abstract, Methods and Results (lines 25 vs 69, 77 vs 91, 25 vs 57). Line 69 says the primary outcome was changed "after review of the 6-month data". If that is true, it is a fatal integrity problem. If it is a drafting error, it is fixable. The desk screen cannot tell which, so referees must adjudicate.
- **Decision:** **Send out.** Desk-reject risk: **medium**. The design and fit justify review, but the primary-outcome contradictions would sink the paper at statistical review if they are not resolved.

## 2. Referee reports (form E)

### Referee 1: clinical relevance and design

**Summary.** Individually randomized 1:1 trial at 12 FQHCs in Ohio. Adults with uncontrolled hypertension (SBP ≥140 mm Hg on ≥1 drug) received 12 months of tailored bilingual SMS (reminders, education, two-way refill prompts escalated to a nurse care coordinator) or monthly general-health messages. The reported primary result is −4.2 mm Hg (95% CI, −6.1 to −2.3) in 12-month office SBP. BP control improved by 8.8 points and PDC ≥80% by 12.5 points. Safety was similar between groups.

**Strengths.** Pragmatic setting with high external relevance. Attention-control comparator. Concealed, stratified allocation. 91% retention. Adherence measured from pharmacy records. Pre-specified subgroups with interaction tests. Fidelity is reported (93.8% of flagged replies answered within 2 business days).

**Major issues**

- **R1-M1: The primary outcome was measured by unmasked staff who also deliver part of the intervention, and the manuscript says the opposite elsewhere.** §2 Outcome Assessment (line 57): BP "was measured by the health center nurse care coordinators, who also enrolled participants and responded to flagged text-message replies and were therefore aware of group assignment." The Abstract (line 25, "measured by masked assessors") and Limitations (line 123, "blood pressure was measured by masked assessors") contradict this. The outcome is office BP, which is susceptible to measurement and observer effects, and the measurers also handle intervention-arm escalations. Detection bias could therefore produce part of a 4 mm Hg difference. *Fix:* state truthfully who measured BP and whether readings were automated and unattended (device auto-averaging, staff out of the room). Add a sensitivity analysis on BP recorded at routine clinic visits by non-study staff (EHR), or on unattended readings if any exist. Correct the Abstract and Limitations. *Owner:* `jama-study-design` (blinding safeguards), with `jama-reporting-standards` for CONSORT blinding.
- **R1-M2: The Conclusions go far beyond the evidence and contradict the Discussion and Abstract.** §4 Conclusions (line 127): messaging "lowered blood pressure enough to prevent strokes and myocardial infarctions, and it should be adopted as standard care for all adults with hypertension in all practice settings, where it can replace pharmacist- and nurse-led adherence programs." The trial measured no clinical events (Abstract line 29: "effect on cardiovascular events remain[s] unknown"). It enrolled only adults with uncontrolled hypertension in FQHCs who own a phone and read English or Spanish (line 49, Limitations line 123). It made no comparison with pharmacist-led programmes, and the Discussion calls messaging "a complement to, rather than a substitute for" intensification (line 117). The Key Points (line 19) call the programme "low-cost", but cost-effectiveness "will be reported separately" (line 123). *Fix:* rewrite the Conclusions to the primary outcome and the trial population. Remove the event, universal-adoption and replacement claims, and drop "low-cost" until cost data exist. *Owner:* `jama-writing-style` (spin), with `jama-scope-fit`.
- **R1-M3: The intervention is a human-plus-automated bundle, and the attention control does not match it.** Methods line 61: flagged replies "generated a task for the health center's nurse care coordinator". Results line 95: 1,262 flagged replies, most about cost or running out of medication. The control arm got 12 messages over 12 months against a median of 418 (line 63 vs line 95). Discussion line 115 nonetheless says the comparison "controlled for the attention of regular contact". The title and Key Points describe the intervention as "automated" text messaging, so readers may attribute to automation an effect that may come from coordinator problem-solving. The same coordinators serve both arms, which also leaves room for contamination. *Fix:* reframe it as SMS with coordinator escalation, report coordinator time per participant, and soften the attention-control claim. *Owner:* `jama-study-design`, with `jama-writing-style`.

**Minor issues.** (a) The mediation claim that the effect "operated mainly through improved adherence" (line 115) rests on two secondary outcomes and should be labelled speculative. (b) BP control is defined as <140/90 (line 69) while the Introduction cites the <130/80 target (line 35). Justify the threshold or report both. (c) Report how many participants needed a coordinator call, and what was done about cost problems. (d) Generalizability to non-phone owners and other languages is acknowledged; give the screening-to-eligibility losses attributable to the phone and language criteria.

**Recommendation:** major revision, conditional on R2's adjudication of the primary outcome. If the outcome was switched after unblinded review, reject.

### Referee 2: statistics

**Summary (statistical).** Primary estimand as reported: the adjusted mean difference in 12-month change in office SBP, from linear regression with stratification factors and baseline SBP and HC-robust SEs. Sample size: 511 per group gives 85% power to detect 3 mm Hg with SD 16, inflated by 15% to 1,202. I re-derived this and it is correct. Missing 12-month outcome: 8.9%.

**Major issues**

- **R2-M1: The primary outcome is stated inconsistently, and one passage describes a data-driven switch.** §2 Outcomes (line 69): "The primary outcome was BP control … this outcome replaced change in office SBP, the primary outcome in the trial registration, after review of the 6-month data." Three other passages say the opposite. Trial Design (line 45) says "no changes were made to eligibility criteria or outcomes after the trial began". The Abstract (line 25) says "The prespecified primary outcome was change in office SBP". Table 2 lists SBP change as primary, and the sample size (line 73) is powered on SBP. Line 85 states that "no interim efficacy analysis was planned or performed", which cannot be reconciled with a switch made after reviewing outcome data at 6 months. At JAMA statistical review this is the decisive question. A post hoc swap of the primary outcome is disqualifying, and a misreported one destroys trust in the rest. *Fix:* report the registered primary (12-month SBP change) as primary, with the registry history (ClinicalTrials.gov archive dates) and the SAP version and date. If any change occurred, disclose when it happened, who had seen which data, and why, and label BP control as secondary. *Owner:* `jama-statistics` (pre-specified outcomes), with `jama-ethics-registration`.
- **R2-M2: The analysis population for the primary analysis is contradictory, and the stated plan is per-protocol.** §2 Statistical Analysis (line 77): "The primary analysis was restricted to the per-protocol population … Participants without a 12-month measurement were excluded; an intention-to-treat analysis with multiple imputation … was not performed." Results (line 91) says "All 1,208 randomized participants were included in the primary analysis". The Table 2 note says all randomized participants were analyzed and "combined across 20 multiply imputed data sets", and Figure 1 says "all 604 per group included in the intention-to-treat analysis". The sensitivity list (line 79) also contradicts line 77: it names "a complete-case analysis" and "a per-protocol analysis" as *sensitivity* analyses, and the tipping-point analysis it describes needs imputed values. A reader cannot tell which population produced −4.2 mm Hg. Per-protocol as primary is also unacceptable for an RCT at JAMA. *Fix:* make ITT with multiple imputation under MAR the primary analysis as the SAP specifies, keep complete-case, MMRM, per-protocol and delta-adjusted analyses as sensitivity, and rewrite line 77. *Owner:* `jama-statistics` (ITT primary; missing data), with `jama-study-design`.
- **R2-M3: The abstract's headline effect does not match the trial's estimate.** Abstract (line 27): "adjusted difference in SBP change, −5.4 mm Hg [95% CI, −6.1 to −2.3]". The body (line 99), Key Points and Table 2 give −4.2 with the same CI, and −5.4 is not even at the centre of that interval. *Fix:* correct it to −4.2. *Owner:* `jama-structured-abstract`. Preflight NUM-MISMATCH already flags this.

**Minor issues.** (a) The tipping-point analysis (line 99) is described relative to "imputed values", so state the imputation model (variables, number of imputations, MAR assumption), consistent with the corrected primary analysis. (b) Give exact interaction P values per subgroup in Figure 2, not only "≥ .19". (c) Report the unadjusted difference alongside the adjusted one. (d) Secondary outcomes are correctly labelled exploratory with no multiplicity adjustment (line 81). Keep their CIs and do not call them "significant" in the Key Points or Discussion. (e) Health centre enters as a fixed stratification covariate, which is appropriate for individual randomization. Note whether a centre random effect or clustering by care coordinator changes the SE, since coordinators span both arms. (f) Fix the Table 2 note: it should say the CIs come from robust SEs combined by Rubin's rules (preflight EXH-SE-NOTE).

**Recommendation:** reject in current form. Major revision if R2-M1 and R2-M2 turn out to be drafting errors and the registry and SAP confirm SBP change as primary and ITT as the primary analysis.

### Referee 3: reporting and ethics

**CONSORT / ethics ledger.** "Randomized" is in the title ✔ · allocation, sequence and concealment ✔ (line 53) · flow diagram ✔ (Figure 1) · registration with a claim of pre-enrolment timing ✔ (line 147) · IRB named, Helsinki, written consent ✔ (line 85) · DSMB ✔ · contributorship and data-access statement ✔ (line 131) · COI and funder role ✔ (lines 135–143) · **data-sharing statement ✘** · structured-abstract headings partly missing ✘ · CONSORT 2010 cited where 2025 is current ✘.

**Major issues**

- **R3-M1: CONSORT item on changes to outcomes after commencement is misreported.** Line 45 says no changes to outcomes, and line 69 describes a change. The CONSORT checklist (Supplement 2) cannot be completed truthfully against this text. *Owner:* `jama-reporting-standards`.
- **R3-M2: The ICMJE/JAMA data-sharing statement is missing.** There is no data-sharing section between line 129 and line 149, and JAMA treats this as a hard gate for trials. *Fix:* add a concrete statement covering what de-identified IPD will be shared, which documents, when, with whom and by what mechanism. *Owner:* `jama-ethics-registration`.
- **R3-M3: CONSORT blinding item is misreported.** The Abstract (line 25) and Limitations (line 123) say "masked assessors". Line 57 says the assessors were aware of assignment. *Owner:* `jama-reporting-standards`.

**Minor issues.** (a) The abstract runs Importance, Objective, Design/Setting/Participants and Interventions together without JAMA's structured headings (line 23). Relabel them (`jama-structured-abstract`). (b) Cite CONSORT 2025 and use its checklist (line 45). (c) Disclose the digital-health company Dr Whitfield advises (line 135) and whether it is involved in text-messaging products. (d) For race and ethnicity, say who classified it and why it was collected (partly done, Table 1 note). (e) The preflight mechanics (the TODO at line 123, the Margolis reference, Table 3/4 numbering, the uncited REDCap reference) must be cleared before submission.

**Recommendation:** major revision. Reporting integrity depends on resolving R3-M1 and R3-M3.
